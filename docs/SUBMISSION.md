**Project:** Saatchi (சாட்சி, "witness")  **Track:** Indie Developer  **Theme:** Build for Your World

**One line:** Checks what a worker says they did, in Tamil or English, against what the video actually shows, and refuses to confirm what it cannot see.

**Problem:** Field work is reported by voice note, and nobody can check it. A report that sounds confident is accepted; a wrong count or wrong order goes unnoticed.

**What it does:** Muse Voice Transcribe turns the narration into timed, speaker-labelled turns (Tamil + English bias). Muse Spark 1.3 extracts atomic claims. Each claim is checked by two independent channels: Spark video reasoning on the claim's time window, and SAM 3.1 object tracks (counts, appearance intervals). A verifier marks it SUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED or UNVERIFIABLE and abstains when the channels disagree or confidence is low. For unverified claims it asks, in Tamil and English, for a short clip that would settle it, then re-checks. Output is a bilingual HTML report.

**Meta tooling used:** Muse Voice Transcribe 1.0, Muse Spark 1.3 (text + video via the Responses API and Files API), SAM 3.1 video concept tracking. Muse Image is not used.

**Evaluation:** Saatchi-Bench, self-filmed clips each with a truthful and falsified narrations, human-labelled. Reported: coverage, accuracy on answered claims, false-confirmation rate (a lie marked SUPPORTED), 95% bootstrap CIs, and an ablation of Spark alone vs Spark+SAM. Results are in the video and RESULTS.md (generated by `python -m saatchi.cli bench`). The sample is small; intervals are wide.

**Prior work:** Claim-versus-video verification exists in research. Our contribution is the application, Tamil/Tanglish input, the two-channel abstaining design, the evidence-request loop and a measured benchmark.

**Limitations:** Small self-filmed benchmark; Voice gives turn-level (not word-level) timestamps; SAM tracks at most 16 objects per frame and can fragment tracks; Tamil/Tanglish accuracy was measured only on our own recordings.
